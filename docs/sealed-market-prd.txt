PRD: SEALED MARKET, PRIVATE DATA MARKET FOR HUMANS AND AGENTS

Source page: https://cursor.com/canvas/store-d0191a84-7d74-4109-8f87-44db0dc2a23f/321d096a-565e-42ae-badc-396dd3ada720

ONE LINE
Sell answers, not datasets. A private AI appraiser running in a NEAR AI Cloud TEE grades the data before anyone pays. Buyers, human or agent, pay per attested answer from any chain, and every contributor gets a Shapley-based share. Drafted 31 Oct 2026 (Africa/Lagos, UTC+1). Anything marked ASSUMPTION is unverified.


1. PROBLEM

Buyers can't judge a dataset's value without seeing it, and once they've seen it they no longer need to buy it. This is Arrow's information paradox, as framed by Li, Yang, Jia and Pang (2025). The authors of a survey of 75 data marketplaces report that sellers mostly offer only "a description of their datasets, a price, sometimes an outdated sample" (Azcoitia, Laoutaris et al., arXiv 2012.08874). Sellers also fear copying, and people who contribute personal data get very little: Vana says typical payouts are "roughly a few dollars" (Axios, 2025-09-22). Agents that need data (Agent Economy track) have no way to check quality before they spend.


2. USERS

- Human seller (individual or data collective)
  Need: Earn from data without handing over a copy; see who queried and why.
- Human buyer (analyst, researcher, startup)
  Need: Know the data is worth the price before paying; get the answer or model metric, not liability for raw data.
- Agent buyer
  Need: Decide automatically from a signed score, stay within a spend cap, pay from any chain.
- Agent seller
  Need: List derived data or a query endpoint and get paid per call.


3. DIFFERENTIATION BACKED BY RESEARCH

DIFFERENTIATORS

- 1. Private AI Appraiser (headline)
  Papers opened: Azcoitia, Laoutaris et al., 'Try Before You Buy', 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08874; Li, Yang, Jia, Pang, 'Data Valuation and Selection in a Federated Model Marketplace', 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18104; Su et al., 'BDTF: blockchain data trading with TEE', 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06813
  Insight and mechanism: TBYB: knowing a buyer's model accuracy on each individual dataset (O(N) information) gets close to optimal purchasing. So the appraiser runs the buyer's task on each dataset inside the TEE and releases only scores. BDTF shows a TEE can serve as the fair exchange; we add attestation the buyer can check plus NEAR escrow.

- 2. Pay per answer, metered privacy
  Papers opened: Koutris, Upadhyaya, Balazinska, Howe, Suciu, 'Query-Based Data Pricing', PODS 2012 / JACM 2015, https://doi.org/10.1145/2770870; Koutris et al., 'Toward practical query pricing with QueryMarket', SIGMOD 2013, https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2463676.2465335; Ghosh and Roth, 'Selling Privacy at Auction', 2010, https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1375
  Insight and mechanism: Sellers price a few views; any query's price is derived so that it is arbitrage-free, and query history prevents double charging. Ghosh and Roth treat privacy loss (differential privacy epsilon) as the thing being sold, so each answer spends a per-seller privacy budget that the seller prices and caps.

- 3. Contributor Shapley split
  Papers opened: Ghorbani and Zou, 'Data Shapley', 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02868; Jia et al., 'Towards Efficient Data Valuation Based on the Shapley Value', 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10275; 'Private Data Valuation and Fair Payment in Data Marketplaces', 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08723; Vincent et al., 'Data Leverage', 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09995
  Insight and mechanism: Shapley is the fair credit rule for pooled data. 2210.08723 does it privately with MPC; we do it in a TEE (simpler, but weaker trust). Revenue flows to each contributor's named account, which gives a data collective the 'leverage' Vincent et al. describe.

- Agent-native buying (supporting)
  Papers opened: Tomasev, Franklin, Leibo, Jacobs et al., 'Virtual Agent Economies', 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10147
  Insight and mechanism: Agent markets need mechanisms built in on purpose. Ours are spend caps via function-call keys and auto-buy rules on attested scores.

- Appraiser limits
  Papers opened: Zheng et al., 'Judging LLM-as-a-Judge', 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685; Koh and Liang, 'Influence Functions', 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04730
  Insight and mechanism: LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases. So numeric scores come from measured task metrics (accuracy delta, duplicates, freshness); the LLM only writes the explanation. Influence functions are a cheaper per-row marginal-value estimate for later.

4. GOALS AND NON-GOALS

1. Goal: a buyer, human or agent, sees an attested appraisal before paying and receives only answers.
2. Goal: the seller's raw data never leaves the TEE, and every query is logged onchain.
3. Goal: a gas-free human seller signs up in under 2 minutes.
4. Non-goal (MVP): regulated personal data (health, KYC), MPC, full differential-privacy proofs, legal licensing templates.
5. Non-goal: hiring agents for jobs (that's market.near.ai).


5. USER STORIES

1. As a seller, I upload a CSV and set a price per answer and a privacy budget, and I never share the file.
2. As a buyer, I state my task ('predict churn' or 'average price by city') and get an attested appraisal score before I pay.
3. As an agent buyer, I auto-buy when score >= 0.7 and price <= my cap, using a function-call key that can't overspend.
4. As a contributor to a pooled dataset, I receive my Shapley share automatically.
5. As a buyer, I get a refund from escrow if the delivered answer's measured quality falls below the appraised range.


6. CORE FLOWS

Step 1 - List: The seller signs in with email and gets a named account through meta-tx. They encrypt the data to the TEE's attested key, so the client checks the attestation before upload. They set views, a price per view and an epsilon budget. The contract records a listing token (NEP-245 class).

Step 2 - Appraise (free or cheap): The buyer submits a task spec. The appraiser in the TEE computes quality (nulls, duplicates, freshness, schema fit, a synthetic-data heuristic), task utility (holdout metric with and without the data, the TBYB individual score) and a fair price range. It returns a report signed by the TEE key plus the attestation. Raw rows are never returned.

Step 3 - Buy: The buyer pays from any chain through intents/1Click into escrow. An agent buyer uses a function-call key that has an allowance. The buyer receives a NEP-245 access token for N answers.

Step 4 - Answer: The query runs in the TEE with a DP-noised aggregate or a model metric. The privacy budget is decremented and the query hash is logged onchain.

Step 5 - Settle: If the delivered quality is within the appraised range, escrow pays out, split among contributors by TEE-computed Shapley shares. Otherwise the buyer is refunded.


7. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

1. FR1: Attestation is verified client-side with a fresh nonce before any upload or query, and a 'Verified' panel is shown.
2. FR2: The appraisal report includes a score in [0,1], sub-scores, a price range, the dataset hash, a task hash, the TEE signature and the attestation quote.
3. FR3: Only aggregates or metrics leave the TEE; queries are rejected when k < 20 rows (ASSUMPTION threshold) or the budget is exhausted.
4. FR4: The escrow contract releases or refunds based on a TEE-signed delivery-quality claim.
5. FR5: Revenue splits go to contributor named accounts by Shapley weight (Monte Carlo permutation sampling, per Ghorbani and Zou).
6. FR6: Agent API: an HTTP plus SKILL.md, and auto-buy rules evaluated on signed reports.


8. NEAR ARCHITECTURE

- Appraiser and query engine
  NEAR primitive: NEAR AI Cloud TEE plus attestation
  Why essential: Seller data stays sealed; the buyer can check the code that graded it
- market.sealed.near contract
  NEAR primitive: Listings, escrow, access tokens (NEP-245), query log
  Why essential: Public, neutral record of every sale and query
- Payments
  NEAR primitive: Intents / 1Click
  Why essential: The buyer pays in any token or chain; the seller receives USDC
- Accounts
  NEAR primitive: Named accounts plus meta-tx; sub-accounts per dataset (ds1.alice.near)
  Why essential: Humans pay no gas; each dataset has its own revenue account
- Agent access
  NEAR primitive: Function-call keys limited to buy/query with an allowance
  Why essential: Agents spend within a cap the owner sets
- TEE identity
  NEAR primitive: TEE public key registered in the contract (ASSUMPTION: verified from attestation off-chain by a relayer in MVP)
  Why essential: Lets the contract trust signed reports


9. DATA MODEL

- Listing: id, seller_account, dataset_hash, schema, views[{name, price_usdc}], epsilon_budget, epsilon_spent, contributors[{account, rows}]
- Appraisal: listing_id, task_hash, score, subscores{quality, freshness, dup_rate, synthetic_flag, utility_delta}, price_low, price_high, tee_sig, attestation_ref
- AccessToken (NEP-245): listing_id, buyer, answers_left, expiry
- QueryLog: token_id, query_hash, epsilon_used, result_hash, ts
- Escrow: amount, buyer, listing_id, appraisal_id, status


10. PRICING AND REVENUE SPLIT

The seller sets prices on views. The appraiser suggests a fair range: price_high = the seller's price; price_low scales with utility_delta (ASSUMPTION: linear for the MVP). The query price follows Koutris-style view pricing, simplified to a per-view charge with history-based de-duplication. Fees: 5% platform fee (ASSUMPTION, modelled on the 5% fee reported for market.near.ai). The remainder is split by Shapley weight across contributors. If the delivered quality is below price_low, the buyer is refunded.


11. PRIVACY MODEL AND ATTESTATION FLOW

1. The client fetches the attestation with a nonce, verifies the measurement, and gets the enclave's public key.
2. The seller encrypts the data to that key; decryption happens only in the TEE. Assumption: the data is held encrypted at rest outside the TEE.
3. Outputs: aggregates with Laplace noise (epsilon per query), model metrics, and appraisal scores. No row-level output.
4. Limits, stated plainly: TEE side-channel and vendor trust; repeated aggregates can leak, which is why the epsilon budget exists; an appraiser can be gamed by sellers who tune data to the scorer, which is mitigated by buyer-supplied holdouts and hidden task specs.

17. RISKS AND OPEN QUESTIONS

1. Cold start and data supply: no sellers means no market. Mitigation: seed with open datasets as 'free appraisal' listings; target one niche first (e.g. local-commerce or research survey data). This is open.
2. Appraiser manipulation: sellers overfit to known checks. Mitigation: buyer-supplied hidden holdout; versioned appraiser code hash in the attestation.
3. LLM judge errors and bias (Zheng et al. 2023): numeric scores come from metrics; the LLM only explains.
4. Unverified NEAR capabilities: whether NEAR AI Cloud runs arbitrary Python or data jobs in the TEE versus only model inference (if only inference, use Outlayer or a TEE we deploy; check day 1). Whether the contract can verify attestation onchain.
5. Legal: data licensing and personal-data law. The MVP uses only fictional or open data.
6. Competition: generic data marketplaces from other teams. Our edge is the attested appraiser plus pay-per-answer plus Shapley; say so in the first 15 seconds.
